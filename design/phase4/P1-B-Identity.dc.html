Direction A · Margin · session 2

Identity

A working document with a margin. The record is written in black; DEIXEN writes in blue, in the margin, level with the line it means. Everything below is built from that: the margin rule, the fielded line, the proof mark.

DEIXEN is a provisional name. The construction does not depend on the letters: any name set on the field line, with the margin rule at its start, keeps the identity.

Wordmark

Construction, cap height 100 units. Dashed lines: the record's cells, one per letter. The field line and its ticks close each cell.

Margin rule

The D's stem is the margin rule, in DEIXEN's blue. It starts above the cap line and runs down into the field line, as the margin runs past every line on the page.

Record

The bowl and E I X E N are record ink, drawn on the Terminal's pitch: each letter owns one cell, like a character on the screen. Stroke 13, square ends.

Field line

The name sits on a form's entry line, one tick per cell: the fielded data of a load sheet. The field line is what makes it a name entered, not a word typeset.

Not a font

Every letter is drawn. Nothing in the wordmark comes from a typeface, so it cannot drift when fonts change.

Signature details

Rule system

Three rules, no boxes: a 2 px ink rule heads a section, hairlines divide rows and fields, one blue rule marks where DEIXEN's margin begins. Only the Terminal is a closed sheet.

Fielded label

The label sits in the field's corner in sentence case; the value is set in its own voice below it. No spaced capitals, no eyebrows.

Leader

A note is joined to its line by a hairline that runs through blank space only and stops at the margin rule. Where no blank path exists (right to left, across a proof mark), the note simply stands level with its line.
